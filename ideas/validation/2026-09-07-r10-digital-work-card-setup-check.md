# 2026-09-07 R10 Validation — Digital Work Card Sector Setup Check

## Candidate
Digital Work Card Sector Setup Check: a fixed-fee, non-legal readiness pre-check for small operators in newly affected sectors.

## Buyer
Greek salons, barber shops, beauty/nail centers, laundries, cleaners/facility services, private health support operators, employment-support businesses and similar small employers entering the 2026 Digital Work Card rollout.

## Evidence
- The Ministry of Labour announced a second phase/pilot from 29 June to 15 November 2026 for additional sectors including health activities, employment support, telecoms, salons/beauty, laundries, cleaning and building/outdoor services.
- Taxheaven/Forin/PIM/EY-style snippets confirm the relevant 2026 decisions/KAD lists and sector expansion.
- Facebook-visible snippets show real confusion: a salon KAD appears to fall under Digital Work Card while Ergani employer data says no; another salon hiring/submission case hits a required-card message during the pilot period.
- Provider competition also validates price/action: Reused advertises Digital Work Card 2026 help from EUR50; TaxPoint/Techbuddy/Taxnerd-style pages sell guides or support and mention fines up to EUR10,500 per employee.
- Accessibility/WCAG was rejected as the parallel category: EAA obligation and EUR300+ agency pricing exist, but agencies sell the exact audit/remediation and the smallest firms may be exempt.

## Offer Boundary
This is not legal, payroll or accounting advice. It is:
- exposure/date/KAD sanity check from public information;
- readiness checklist for Ergani/app/provider setup;
- screenshot-based "what appears configured/missing" review;
- one-page employee clock-in/clock-out SOP;
- exact questions to send to accountant/software provider.

## 48-Hour Gate
1. Build a list of 10 named affected businesses with public contact path: five salons/beauty, three cleaners/laundries, two private health/support operators.
2. Send or draft a EUR49-EUR79 pre-check offer with non-legal boundaries.
3. Ask one accountant or software provider whether they would refer overflow setup-readiness checks.
4. Kill if there are no replies, if all answers route back to the accountant/provider, or if operators will not share enough screenshots/info safely.

## Kill Conditions
- Direct replies say "accountant/software provider handles all of it."
- No safe way to review setup without sensitive payroll/account credentials.
- Provider/agency pricing makes the pre-check too cheap to trust or too close to regulated advice.
- No ten reachable targets in newly affected sectors.

## Learning
Regulatory timing is only useful when paired with a named operator segment and a narrow safe action. The idea is the pre-check and accountant handoff, not "become their payroll consultant."
